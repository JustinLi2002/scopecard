How to read a scope card

A card compares a benchmark release with a declared universe: the population the benchmark is meant to stand for. It reports what the release can never evaluate. It needs no labels and no models, so it cannot say how models would score on the excluded part; it says how large that difference would have to be to matter.

Scope statement

One sentence: the share the benchmark leaves out, of a population the sentence names with its size (for WN18RR, the 112,199 WordNet 3.0 synsets that carry at least one triple of its 11 relations), and whether a simple rule explains the release. The card's W is a share of line repeats that population, and Definitions states how the universe and depth were built. Claims made on the benchmark are licensed for the part it covers.

Warnings and input files

A card lists, under Warnings and again after the scope statement, anything a reader must check before using it: only part of the release's ids occur in the universe (between 50% and 95%; below 50% the tool refuses to write a card, because the files do not look like a release of that universe), lines of a raw file that did not have the expected format and were skipped, or a pair file with a label column that was not used. Input files gives the lines read and skipped per file read leniently (raw universes, qrels, pair tables). Release files have a fixed format: a line that does not fit stops the audit with the file name and line number.

Mapping

How release ids land on universe units. Unmapped ids are absent from the universe. Merged ids stand for more than one unit: WN18RR keys synsets by bare WordNet offsets, which synsets of different parts of speech share, so 161 of its ids each stand for more than one synset. Unmapped release units count against every rule in the rule diagnosis.

Candidates: exclusion or coverage

A unit missing from a release is excluded only if the benchmark could never evaluate it. Each card says how the benchmark chooses its evaluation candidates. When candidates are enumerated over the release's units (every entity in link prediction, every item in all-item ranking, every judged topic), a unit absent from the release is never evaluated, and W is an excluded share. When the release only lists pairs drawn by an unstated rule (pair classification), an absent unit or pair may still have had a chance of being drawn; the card then reports coverage and says so, and gives no break-even.

Exclusion (W)

W is the excluded share of the universe: units for knowledge graphs and query-level benchmarks, unobserved user–item pairs for recommendation. For query-level benchmarks with relevance judgments, the card also gives, inside each evaluated query, the share of the corpus that was never judged and is therefore scored as non-relevant.

Depth profile and derived scope

Depth is how much source evidence a unit has in the universe, for example the number of facts on an entity. The profile shows whether exclusion follows depth (a rarity cut) or not. The derived scope d_min is the smallest depth threshold whose restricted universe has W at or below 0.5. When no threshold gets there, the card says so.

Rule diagnosis

The rule is never an input. The tool searches simple rule families on depth (single threshold, both ends above a threshold, seeds plus their neighbours, iterated k-core) and reports the best threshold of each with its Jaccard overlap with the release. reproduced means Jaccard 1, approximately reproduced at least 0.95, not reproduced below. A stated rule from the paper is checked the same way. A best threshold at the top of the searched range is marked, because a higher one may fit better.

Stated rule check (query level)

Some rules can be checked only on the released units, because excluded units have no published data (TREC topics that were never judged). The card then says whether every released unit satisfies the rule and that the excluded side cannot be checked.

Comparison (break-even)

For a difference ΔO between two models on the benchmark, the difference on the universe is (1 − W) ΔO + W ΔU, where ΔU is their difference on the excluded part. The ordering reverses exactly when ΔU has the opposite sign and exceeds (1 − W) |ΔO| / W, or, equivalently, when the change between the regions, c = ΔO − ΔU, has the sign of ΔO and its magnitude exceeds c* = |ΔO| / W. The card gives both thresholds. They hold for metrics that are linear in the candidate mix, such as per-query AUC with a fixed positive reference. With per-query shares, W is their mean, and c* is exact for a ΔO measured on the same test queries under the same filter; for a difference from another query set or filter, or a single W where the shares vary between queries, the thresholds carry no guarantee. MRR and Hits@k are not linear in the mix, so the card gives no threshold for them. ΔU and c need labels on the excluded part and are not computed.

Hygiene

Checks that do not depend on the universe: for knowledge graphs, test triples whose reverse is a training triple (answered by one training edge through a symmetric relation), broken down by relation, and test entities unseen in training; for sampled negatives, whether any negative is later than the query or cited by it, and whether sampling is uniform over nodes.
